Why it matters

Plastic Pollution:
A Multi-Front Crisis

From marine ecosystems to human health, the damage of unchecked plastic waste reaches further than most people realize.

🐋
1M+
Marine Animals Killed Yearly

Over a million seabirds and marine mammals die each year from plastic ingestion or entanglement in discarded fishing gear and packaging waste.

🧬
5g
Microplastics We Ingest Weekly

Humans consume approximately a credit card's worth of microplastic particles every week through water, food, and air — with long-term health consequences still unfolding.

🌡️
3.4%
Of Global CO₂ Emissions

Plastic production and incineration contribute significantly to greenhouse gas emissions, making plastic pollution also a major climate crisis.

🪸
89%
Of Coral Reefs Affected

Research shows that contact with plastic raises the likelihood of disease in coral from 4% to 89%, devastating the foundation of marine biodiversity.

💧
83%
Of Tap Water Contaminated

Studies across 14 countries found microplastics in 83% of tap water samples, putting drinking water safety at risk for billions of people.

🌍
$13B
Annual Economic Damage

The annual cost of marine plastic pollution to fisheries, tourism, and coastal communities exceeds $13 billion — borne disproportionately by vulnerable communities.

ABOUT
Our story

We Believe in a
Plastic-Free Planet

PlastiZero was founded in 2018 by a group of marine biologists, engineers, and activists who witnessed firsthand the devastation of plastic pollution on ocean ecosystems. What started as beach cleanups became a global movement.

Today, we operate in 47 countries, have removed over 12,000 tonnes of plastic from natural environments, and influence legislation in three continents.

Science-Driven

Every initiative we launch is grounded in peer-reviewed research. We partner with leading universities and research institutions to ensure our approach is evidence-based and measurable.

Community First

Real change happens at the local level. We empower communities with tools, training, and resources — respecting cultural contexts while pursuing universal goals.

How we got here

A Timeline of Plastic Crisis

1907

Bakelite — First Synthetic Plastic

Leo Baekeland invents the world's first fully synthetic plastic. It is hailed as a revolutionary material with unlimited potential.

0 tonnes of ocean plastic
1950s

Mass Production Begins

Post-war economic boom drives explosive growth in single-use plastic packaging. Disposability becomes a design philosophy and marketing virtue.

2M tonnes/year produced
1972

First Ocean Plastics Study

Scientists publish the first evidence of floating plastic debris in the Sargasso Sea — a warning largely ignored by industry and policymakers.

First detected ocean accumulation
1997

Great Pacific Garbage Patch Discovered

Oceanographer Charles Moore documents a vast gyre of plastic debris twice the size of Texas in the North Pacific — shocking the world.

80,000 tonnes in one gyre alone
2015

Microplastics Crisis Identified

Research reveals microplastics have infiltrated every layer of the food chain — from deep-sea fish to Arctic ice cores — creating a permanent global contamination.

5 trillion plastic pieces floating now
2022

Plastics Found in Human Blood

Dutch scientists detect microplastics in the blood of 77% of test subjects — proving plastic has crossed into the human body for the first time.

400M+ tonnes produced that year

Technology spotlight

Innovations We're Scaling

01
🤖

OceanBot Autonomous Drones

Solar-powered underwater drones that navigate autonomously, identify plastic waste using computer vision, and collect up to 500kg per day. Currently 180 units deployed globally.

02
🧫

Plastic-Eating Enzymes

Supporting biotech lab development of engineered enzymes (based on the 2016 Ideonella sakaiensis discovery) that break down PET plastic into reusable components within days.

03
🛰️

Satellite Pollution Tracking

Partnership with ESA for satellite imaging to map ocean plastic accumulation zones at continental scale — enabling faster targeted cleanup deployment.

04
🏗️

Plastic-to-Construction

Converting non-recyclable mixed plastics into construction materials — bricks, tiles, and road surface materials — giving waste a second life while solving housing material shortages.

05
📱

PlastiScan AI App

Mobile app that lets anyone scan packaging to see its recyclability, find nearest drop-off points, and contribute to our global plastic pollution database with crowdsourced data.

06
🌊

Coastal Interceptor Barriers

Passive floating barriers placed at river exits that funnel plastic waste into collection systems using natural water currents — no energy required. 93% collection efficiency.
